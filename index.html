About Me

I am a biomedical engineer who is interested in tissue engineering and regenerative medicine.
My previous work mainly involved biomaterials such as hydrogels. With a multidisciplinary background, I also carried out experiments about fermentation engineering and finite element analysis as well. My current project focused on the differentiation of human PSCs and the immunomodulation and quality control of MSCs.

Projects

    Tissue-Engineering-Oriented Research on Early Fate Decision of PSCs We have noticed that the current in vitro differentiation strategies of human pluripotent stem cells (PSCs) exhibit certain level of heterogeneity. Therefore, we want to investigate whether functional cells obtained by different induction routes have different functions and therapeutic potential in the context of the endodermal lineage.
    Research on Key Pathways of In Vitro Induction and In Vivo Genesis of MSCs This study aims to develop a chemically defined method for obtaining mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) from pluripotent stem cells (PSCs) through different germ layers and investigates the key components in traditional serum culture medium that are essential for in vitro induction of MSCs.
    Effect of Culture Conditions on Therapeutic Immunomodulation Ability of MSCs: A QC Study We want to explore what pathways and regulatory mechanisms ultimately affect the therapeutic efficacy of MSCs, especially their immune regulatory ability and in vivo survival after transplantation.
    This research is part of the project under the National Key R&D Program led by Prof. Xu Renhe.
    FEA Insights Into the Mechanics of hPSC Colonies Human PSC colonies exhibit a remarkable epithelial-mesenchymal gradient of morphology from the periphery to the center, which reflects a complex interplay of the microenvironment, signaling pathways, cell adhesion, and mechanisms. We aim to employ finite element analysis to create an in silico platform that can overcome the limitations of traditional traction force microscopy, in order to investigate the mechanical properties of pluripotent stem cell colonies and understand the reason of such unique colony morphology.
